Cross-Implementation Interoperability Results
=============================================

.. rubric:: LUNALINK-INTEROP-001

**Date:** 2026-03-24 (first run), updated 2026-08-31

**Implementations under test:**

- **LunaLink** v1.0 — C++ / Python LSIS-AFS reference implementation
- **LANS-AFS-SIM** (Ebinuma) — C, BSD-2-Clause, https://github.com/osqzss/LANS-AFS-SIM

LANS-AFS-SIM is a transmit-side simulator: it exposes
``encode_LDPC_AFS_SF2``/``SF3`` and no decode, acquisition or tracking entry
point.  That shapes what can be compared.  Encoding is deterministic, so
bit-exact agreement is the complete comparison and is what L1–L2 check.
Decoding is statistical, so it is characterised rather than compared — and
only LunaLink has a decoder to characterise, which is why the receiver
measurements in :doc:`signal/ldpc_algo_card` are LunaLink's shipped decoder
against LunaLink's own unhardened float64 reference decoder and the
finite-length sphere-packing bound, not against LANS-AFS-SIM.  It is also
why the reverse direction (L4–L5) names PocketSDR-AFS rather than
LANS-AFS-SIM: closing it needs a third-party *receiver*.

**Reference:** Sobukawa & Ebinuma, *Aerospace* 12(7), 2025

.. note::

   **To reproduce every result on this page:** ``task interop``

   It clones and builds LANS-AFS-SIM on first run, then executes the
   cross-checks below.  No manual setup, no credentials, no vendored
   third-party source in this repository.

Levels 4–5: reverse direction and parsing
-----------------------------------------

The results above cover levels 1–3 of the competition's five-level
interoperability plan.  Levels 4 (an *independent receiver* decoding
LunaLink-generated signals — PocketSDR-AFS, Ebinuma's software-defined
receiver) and 5 (navigation-data parsing cross-checks) are the reverse
direction.

**Both are now demonstrated.**  ``task interop-receiver`` builds
`PocketSDR-AFS <https://github.com/osqzss/PocketSDR-AFS>`__ — Sobukawa and
Ebinuma's software-defined LANS AFS receiver, the receiving counterpart to

Method
~~~~~~

LunaLink generates three consecutive 12-second frames (PRN 3, pseudo-random
payload, seed ``0x12345678``) at 12 MHz, adds AWGN at 45 dB-Hz and quantises to
the four-level ``{-3,-1,+1,+3}`` int8 I/Q that LANS-AFS-SIM's ``-b 2`` mode
writes and ``pocket_trk`` reads.  Three frames rather than one because
acquisition consumes part of the first, so a complete frame must follow it.
The receiver runs with upstream's own invocation from ``pocket_trk.sh``.

``sdr_nav.c`` logs the first 80 hex characters of the 1200 decoded bits and,
separately, characters 294–299 — the CRC-24.  Both are compared against
LunaLink's own frame::

   transmitted SB2: 4850D19D04E4308D820462770927D9CA…  crc=A939B3
   decoded     SB2: 4850D19D04E4308D820462770927D9CA…  crc=A939B3

Reproduce with ``task interop-receiver``; the result is committed as
``docs/assurance/evidence/interop_l4_receiver.json`` and asserted by
``tests/test_interop_receiver.py``.

Stated limits of this demonstration
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

*  **One upstream patch is applied to build the receiver.**
   ``src/sdr_pvt_afs.c`` declares a file-static ``satpos()`` that collides
   with RTKLIB's public ``satpos()``, which Clang rejects.
   ``build_pocketsdr_afs.py`` renames it to ``afs_satpos``.  The patch changes
   no arithmetic and no decision logic; without it the receiver does not
   compile here.
*  **SB2 is the subframe compared.**  It is the one the receiver logs in
   decoded form.
*  The signal is generated at baseband and quantised as above rather than
   captured over the air; no RF hardware is in the loop.

The vectors are maintained in the public test-vector repository, which
ships all five levels with bundled oracles and a zero-dependency validator:

   https://github.com/luar-space/lsis-afs-test-vectors

Its ``codes/`` (210 × ``codes_prnNNN.hex``), ``frames/``, ``signals/``
(12-second I/Q per PRN), and ``parsed/`` directories follow the repository
structure defined in the interoperability plan, and every vector there is
regenerable from this repository via ``scripts/export_test_vectors_all.py``.

Methodology
-----------

**L1 — Spreading code comparison**

A C harness (``dump_lans_codes.c``) calls LANS-AFS-SIM's ``icodegen()`` and
``qcodegen()`` to extract Gold-2046 and Weil-10230 codes for PRN 1–210, then
compares every chip against LunaLink's packed PRN tables.  Both implementations use independent code generation:
LANS-AFS-SIM uses an 11-bit LFSR with a delay table; LunaLink uses
pre-computed tables generated from the LSIS V1.0 Appendix C/D parameters.

**L2 — Frame encoding comparison**

A C harness (``dump_lans_frame.c``) invokes LANS-AFS-SIM's encoding pipeline
(``generate_BCH_AFS_SF1``, ``append_CRC24``, ``encode_LDPC_AFS_SF2/SF3``,
``interleave_AFS_SF234``) for five frame cases spanning three fill patterns
(all-zeros, all-ones, alternating) plus boundary FID/TOI values
(FID=1/TOI=0 and FID=3/TOI=99; ``tests/test_interop_frames.py``).  Each
resulting 6000-symbol frame is compared against LunaLink's ``frame_build()``
output.

**L3 — Cross-implementation signal decode**

LANS-AFS-SIM generates a 25-second composite I/Q signal at 5.115 MHz
containing 7 SVs (PRN 2–8) with Keplerian ephemeris from a lunar NRHO-like
constellation.  The signal is decimated to 1.023 MSPS (1 sample/chip), acquired with
FFT-based PCPS, despread with PLL carrier tracking, and the recovered
symbols are decoded with :func:`lunalink.afs.receive_frame`: sync search,
deinterleaving, LDPC + BCH decode, FID/TOI and WN/ITOW extraction, and
CRC-24 verification on all three subframes (``tests/test_interop_decode.py``).

Known limitations
-----------------

**Demodulation sample rate.** The L3 demodulation chain operates at
1.023 MSPS (1 sample per I-channel chip).  This decimates the composite
signal from its native bandwidth (set by AFS-Q at 5.115 Mcps), aliasing the
Q-channel pilot as broadband interference on the I channel.  Under low-SNR
conditions (2-bit ADC, multiple SVs) this aliasing degrades acquisition
sensitivity; for the clean LANS-AFS-SIM scenario it does not prevent a
correct decode.  A flight-credible receiver would operate at the composite
signal rate (≥ 10.23 MSPS) and acquire on the data-free AFS-Q pilot with
coherent integration over the secondary code period (8 ms) or longer —
receiver design beyond the demodulation needed to demonstrate cross-decode
is outside the scope of this reference implementation.
